Behind the Scenes: How a Hair System Is Made

Most companies in this space treat their manufacturing process like a trade secret. We think you deserve to know exactly what you are paying for, so here is a look at how your hair system goes from raw material to finished product.

It starts with sourcing. We work with a small number of vetted suppliers who provide 100% human hair, ethically sourced and graded for consistency. Every batch is inspected for quality, color accuracy, and tensile strength before it enters production. Hair that does not meet our standards gets rejected — no exceptions.

Next comes base construction. Each base is hand-built to our specifications — the lace is woven, the poly is cast, and hybrid bases are assembled in layers. This is where the engineering happens. Base thickness, knot density, hairline construction, and ventilation patterns are all set during this phase. A single base takes 3-5 days to construct by hand.

Then comes ventilation — the process of individually knotting each hair strand into the base. This is the most labor-intensive step and the one that determines how natural the system looks and moves. A skilled technician ties 40,000 to 60,000 individual knots per unit. At our production facility, the most experienced ventilators have been doing this work for over a decade.

Finally, quality control. Every unit is inspected against 12 checkpoints before it ships: color accuracy, density consistency, knot security, base integrity, hairline gradation, and more. Units that fail any checkpoint go back for rework or get scrapped. Our QC rejection rate runs around 8%, which is higher than the industry average — and that is intentional.

The result is a system you can trust from the moment you open the box. That is the standard, and we are not interested in lowering it.
