When a system gets noticed, it's almost never the hair. It's the line the hair starts on.

Three giveaways, in order of how often we see them:

1. Too straight. Real hairlines are irregular. A ruler-clean front edge is the single fastest tell there is.
2. Too dense. The front of a real head of hair is its lightest zone — fine hairs first, fuller behind. A front that starts at full density starts like a wall.
3. Too low. Grown men don't have the hairline they had at 17. A low, closed hairline on a 45-year-old face doesn't read young — it reads wrong.

None of these are fixed by spending more. All of them are fixed on the spec sheet.

[Text - Base type guidance]
What to actually ask for:

- A soft, irregular front — it's a standard spec-sheet option, not a custom favour.
- Graded density at the hairline — finest knots and lightest hair at the leading edge, stepping up behind.
- Age-appropriate placement — a rough rule: about four fingers above the brow ridge, with slight temple recession. If you have photos of yourself from before, send them with your consultation — they beat any formula.
- Lace at the front if detection is your top concern — it's what the mesh construction is for.

[FAQ]
Should the knots be bleached? — At the hairline, usually yes on darker hair — it removes the dot pattern at the roots. Elsewhere it trades durability for nothing you can see.
Can you copy my old hairline? — Yes. Photos from before are the best spec input we get.
What if my last system's front looked fake? — Almost always one of the three above. Reply with a photo and we'll tell you which.
